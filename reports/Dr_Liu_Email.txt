Subject: AgentQO update: first real-model results

Dear Dr. Liu,

I wanted to share a progress update on AgentQO. Before using the Chameleon allocation, I ran the first real-model experiments on my laptop (Qwen2.5 1.5B and 7B via Ollama, GSM8K, two multi-agent workflows). A short summary and the full reports are attached.

The main results:

1. Criticality varies strongly across agents, but not by graph position. In a math workflow, a verifier that re-reads the problem and can rewrite the answer becomes the most critical agent, while the planner's errors stop mattering. Replacing the verifier with a code passthrough, on identical cached model outputs, makes every upstream agent critical again and restores the structural ordering. Structure predicts criticality only when no agent can repair upstream errors.

2. Spending by criticality works at the level of upgrade sets. In a pre-registered test on 100 problems not used to measure criticality, upgrading the two most critical agents recovered 52% of the full upgrade's accuracy gain for 40% of the extra cost, and beat the structurally chosen pair at equal cost by 26 points (95% CI [+14, +38]; 34 problems won, 8 lost).

3. Three findings changed the design. Where errors hurt most is not where a bigger model helps most: upgrade value rises along the chain while error consequence falls. Upgrades interact strongly, so allocation should be a joint choice over sets rather than a per-agent ranking. And a noisy repair step cancelled every upgrade, so turning a step off can matter more than a bigger model, which supports the joint logical and physical direction.

These are pilot results with small quantized models. I would like to repeat the strongest experiments at scale on Chameleon with vLLM, and I have a setup plan ready.
